I am an **Assistant Research Scientist in Mechanical Engineering at the University of Michigan**. I study how manufacturing conditions set the internal structure of **lithium-ion battery electrodes** and **metal additive-manufactured parts**, and how that structure in turn determines their performance and reliability. My goal is to make these processes predictable: to identify the mechanisms behind defects and variability, and to give engineers physics-based guidance for choosing process settings instead of relying on trial and error.

### Research projects

**Battery electrode manufacturing** — _how drying conditions make or break electrode quality_<br>
Drying decides whether an electrode ends up with a uniform binder distribution or with binder gradients, cracking, and debonding. I revealed the drying mechanism in industrial roll-to-roll electrode manufacturing, showed how drying conditions create binder gradients in the 3D electrode structure, and helped quantify how laser penetration governs fast laser-assisted drying. These results give manufacturers a physics-based basis for selecting drying settings. Conducted with General Motors Global R&D.<br>
Related projects: [electrode drying]({{ '/projects/1_project/' | relative_url }}) · [particle-scale drying]({{ '/projects/5_project/' | relative_url }}) · [solid-state batteries]({{ '/projects/3_project/' | relative_url }})

**Metal additive manufacturing** — _what controls the grain structure of printed metal parts_<br>
Grain structure governs the strength and anisotropy of printed parts, yet it is difficult to predict or control. I showed that competing solute trapping among alloying elements drives nonmonotonic grain selection in laser powder bed fusion, and built a physics-informed machine-learning approach that reduces the most expensive thermodynamic calculations by roughly two orders of magnitude while preserving the governing physics. My doctoral work traced the same process upstream, from powder delivery to melt-pool solidification. DARPA SURGE collaboration with Texas A&M University and AlphaSTAR.<br>
Related projects: [grain-structure prediction]({{ '/projects/2_project/' | relative_url }}) · [powder flow and melt pool]({{ '/projects/6_project/' | relative_url }})

**Computational frameworks** — _making simulation fast and reliable enough to guide engineering decisions_<br>
These questions cross length scales and physical processes, so I build frameworks that link them and can be reused for new materials and processes: coupling deformation with recrystallization in gradient-grained metals, connecting electrochemistry and mechanics in solid-state batteries, and optimizing external pressure in pouch-cell manufacturing. Machine-learning surrogates then turn these expensive simulations into fast design tools.<br>
Related projects: [deformation and recrystallization]({{ '/projects/4_project/' | relative_url }}) · [solid-state battery multiphysics]({{ '/projects/3_project/' | relative_url }})
